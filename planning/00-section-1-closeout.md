# Section 1 Closeout — Product Vision and Problem Brief

**Lifecycle status:** APPROVED.  
**Recorded:** 2026-09-20.  
**Purpose:** Consolidate the approved direction and recorded preferences from D-001 through D-038 into a short reviewable product definition before moving to Section 2.

## Product vision

Build a recommendation-led travel planning product that helps travelers discover what they actually want, organize those choices into a realistic trip, and use the same trip comfortably on larger-screen web and mobile.

The app should reduce the amount of blank-page planning users must do themselves. It should proactively surface useful ideas, explain why they fit, let the user react, and refine the next recommendations while preserving accepted decisions.

## Primary user and use case

The primary use case is an organizer planning a trip they are also taking and sharing it with companions.

The first real-world pilot is a two-person Japan trip. The pilot does not define the permanent market as couples-only, Japan-only, or two-person-only.

## Core user problems

The owner reports difficulty:
- deciding what is actually worth doing,
- deciding where to go first,
- coordinating hotels, travel, reservations, and dates,
- finding date-specific events,
- balancing main experiences with shopping, food, rest, and spontaneity,
- and beginning at all when too many variables are unresolved.

## Core product principles

### 1. Recommendations lead; the traveler decides
The app should propose concrete options rather than require users to invent every destination, hotel, activity, or route themselves.

### 2. Recommendation quality comes first
The highest-priority outcome is recommendations that users genuinely want to add to their trip.

### 3. Same-day discovery is a first-version capability
Travelers should be able to reopen the app during the trip and find worthwhile nearby experiences/events that fit remaining time and commitments.

### 4. Free time is intentional
The itinerary must not automatically fill every gap. A valid day can contain one or two main experiences, food/shopping/rest, and optional spontaneous discovery later.

### 5. Main cities first, optional expansions afterward
For the Japan pilot, establish the core Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka itinerary and booking needs before suggesting extra cities. More generally, optional detours should be introduced after the main plan is coherent.

### 6. Multi-source recommendations
Use a mix of traveler/community reports, Reddit/forums, reviews, local/local-publication sources, calendars, and official/primary sources.

Community/local sources help discover what is appealing. Official/current sources verify logistics such as hours, prices, ticket windows, and access.

### 7. Evidence must be honest
Do not label something as "locals love it," "available," "booked," "live price," or similar unless the evidence supports it.

### 8. Web and mobile are both first-class
Larger-screen web should support rich visual planning, comparisons, maps, photos, and itinerary organization. Mobile should support focused on-trip use, nearby discovery, directions, reservation details, and quick decisions.

The product should maintain consistent trip state and meaning across form factors.

## Hotel experience

The app should:
- offer at least 4–5 meaningful hotel candidates per city when sufficient inventory exists,
- support configurable price ranges,
- support value and comfort/upscale tiers,
- show hotel and room photos,
- show room and property amenities,
- show nearby transportation and relevant itinerary points on a map,
- explain itinerary-specific pros and cons,
- support user amenity preferences,
- distinguish room-specific amenities from property-wide amenities,
- distinguish in-room washer/dryer from shared laundry and paid laundry service,
- and explain what the traveler gains or gives up by paying more.

For the pilot, the current lodging priority is clean, reliable/safe-feeling, price-efficient, convenient, one large bed, and useful amenities. The personal value tier targets roughly USD 100–200 per room/night, while a higher comfort/upscale comparison tier remains available.

## Activity and itinerary experience

The app should support:
- must-do items,
- interested/kept items,
- suggested items,
- booked/confirmed items,
- reservation-aware states,
- flexible versus fixed items,
- hotel and transportation context,
- provider excursions and app-assembled combinations,
- local/cultural experiences,
- shorter and longer experiences,
- and optional same-day additions without silently rewriting the trip.

Exact labels and controls remain Section 3 work.

## Reservation awareness

The app should distinguish at least the concepts:
- booking opens later,
- book now / limited inventory,
- optional reservation,
- no reservation needed,
- booked / confirmed,
- schedule conflict,
- current-year details not yet published.

The product should not imply that a suggestion is a reservation or payment.

## Hotels and transportation

First-version planning includes both:
- organizing existing bookings,
- and recommending hotels/transport before booking.

In-app purchasing, cancellation, modification, email import, and provider integrations remain open and are not automatically part of the first release.

## Sharing / companion use

Sharing remains part of the product concept: the organizer should be able to present and share a useful itinerary with companions.

Detailed companion permissions, editing, accounts, invitation model, and privacy are intentionally deferred to Section 2.

## First-release promise — approval candidate

> Help a traveler move from trip basics to a useful, personalized, shareable itinerary by proactively recommending worthwhile destinations, hotels, experiences, and day structures; explaining the tradeoffs; tracking reservation needs; preserving free time; and supporting current nearby discovery during the trip across web and mobile.

The first release should favor a complete, useful end-to-end planning experience over broad but shallow coverage.

## Important non-goals / not yet approved

The following are not approved first-release requirements unless later selected:
- in-app hotel or restaurant payment,
- full airline booking,
- unrestricted group editing,
- automatic whole-trip rewriting,
- continuous GPS/background tracking,
- notification monitoring,
- guide marketplace,
- social network,
- universal worldwide coverage,
- complicated group voting,
- fully autonomous purchasing,
- guaranteed live inventory from every provider.

## Business direction

The long-term goal is a commercial app-store/SaaS product with subscription and advertising revenue.

The Japan trip remains the immediate pilot. Commercial distribution, pricing, billing, ad placement, and acquisition strategy are later decisions.

## Resource constraints

- Build/design should remain free-first where suitable.
- Paid services require explicit review and approval.
- The owner is willing to devote time as necessary, but no unlimited-capacity assumption should be made.
- November 10, 2026 remains the first complete software-test target, not a booking deadline.

## Evidence status

Confirmed product choices and owner preferences are extensive, but the product has not yet demonstrated:
- market demand,
- recommendation-quality thresholds,
- companion usability,
- live provider coverage,
- real operating cost,
- revenue viability,
- or working software acceptance.

Those are later validation questions, not reasons to keep Section 1 open indefinitely.
