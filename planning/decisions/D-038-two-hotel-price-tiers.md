# D-038 — Two hotel price tiers; clean, safe, price-efficient becomes the primary lodging goal

**Status:** CONFIRMED product and trip direction; exact hotel selections and dated rates remain OPEN.  
**Recorded:** 2026-09-20.  
**Owner:** Project owner (`fgzmac`).  
**Source:** The owner wants to preserve the existing upscale/value tier while also creating lower-priced hotel selections in the USD 100–200 range. The new personal priority is a clean, safe, price-efficient hotel.  
**Related:** D-030 recommendation-led planning; D-031 prior personal search reference; D-033 amenities; D-036 hotel comparison cards/maps/tradeoffs; D-037 web/mobile first-class.  
**Blueprint:** Section 1 remains DRAFT. This changes the owner's hotel-ranking priorities without removing the existing comparison tier.

## Confirmed lodging strategy

Maintain **two hotel comparison tiers** for each city when suitable inventory exists:

### Tier A — Value / price-efficient
Target approximately **USD 100–200 per room per night** for the owner's trip.

Primary goals:
- Clean
- Safe
- Strong enough location to support the itinerary
- One large bed where available
- Good transit access
- Useful amenities
- Strong value

Do not assume a lower-priced hotel is inferior simply because it lacks luxury branding.

### Tier B — Comfort / upscale comparison
Retain the previously approved higher search reference and stronger amenity/room/location options.

Use this tier to answer:
- What would spending more buy?
- Is the better room, location, onsen/laundry, or convenience worth the upgrade?
- Is a higher price justified for a short high-impact stay?

The owner can choose a value hotel in one city and an upscale hotel in another. Do not force one tier across the entire trip.

## New ranking priority for the owner's trip

Hotel ranking should prioritize:

1. Cleanliness
2. Safety / reliable property and neighborhood context
3. Price efficiency
4. Convenient transit and practical location for the actual itinerary
5. One large bed
6. Useful amenities
7. Extra luxury / design

This supersedes an interpretation that "upscale" should dominate the owner's hotel ranking. Upscale remains desirable only when the additional value is worth the added cost.

## Average-price context

Hotel averages vary materially by city, month, hotel segment, and whether the source reports transacted ADR or listed median prices.

Current market context used in this update:
- Tokyo business-hotel research estimates autumn 2026 transacted ADR around JPY 15,200–16,300, while centrally located listed rates for two guests can be substantially higher, especially on Saturdays.
- Kyoto's official DMO reported November 2025 major-hotel ADR of JPY 29,085; November is a high-demand autumn-foliage month.
- Osaka 2026 city/business ADR has generally been lower than Tokyo/Kyoto, and current November/December listing medians are materially lower than Tokyo.

These are market benchmarks, not the owner's dated booking quotes.

## App requirement

For each city, show both:
- **Value tier**
- **Comfort/upscale tier**

Allow users to choose:
- preferred price range,
- hard maximum versus flexible range if later approved,
- desired hotel quality/comfort,
- must-have amenities,
- location tradeoffs.

The app should never equate cheaper with unsafe or lower quality without evidence.

## Pros/cons for value hotels

Useful examples:
- **Pro:** Saves $90/night while keeping direct subway access.
- **Pro:** Clean, highly rated, and walkable to the planned shopping district.
- **Con:** Smaller room and fewer hotel amenities.
- **Con:** Requires a train ride for casual nightlife.
- **Con:** Lower rate is non-refundable.
- **Con:** Great value, but preferred bed type is not available.

## Current Japan planning implication

Expand the Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka hotel shortlist so each city includes:
- 4–5 meaningful value candidates targeting USD 100–200 when available,
- 4–5 comfort/upscale candidates from the prior tier.

Exact-date pricing must still be verified before selection. Kyoto's late-November nights may make the value tier harder to satisfy; the app should explain the market constraint rather than silently move the budget.

No hotel is selected or booked by this decision.
