Include **recommendations for lesser-known cities when specific worthwhile attractions, events, or experiences make a visit relevant**. This is a product requirement, not just a request to include an extra city in this one trip.

## Proposed product principle

> Recommend the experience first, explain why its destination is worth considering, and make the extra travel, time, cost, and booking implications clear.

Lesser-known is not itself a quality score, a claim that a place is undiscovered, or a rule to avoid popular destinations. A candidate must still fit the user's interests and practical constraints. A worthwhile farther destination may justify the effort; proximity alone must not decide. No extra-city quota, maximum hotel count, source provider, or numerical ranking formula is selected.

### Proposed information for a destination recommendation

| Question | Information to show when supported |
| --- | --- |
| Why go? | The specific experience or event and why it fits the traveler. |
| Why during this trip? | Relevant event occurrence or seasonal context; distinguish confirmed dates, annual patterns, and unknowns. |
| How does it fit? | Possible day trip, overnight, stop on the route, or replacement for another stay; alternatives remain proposals. |
| What is the cost of adding it? | Additional travel time, realistic transport, lodging changes, total time and cost, and what it would displace. |
| What must be arranged? | Reservations, entry conditions, booking windows, and unknown availability. |

Avoid filling the trip with new hotel changes or sacrificing all free time merely to show more destinations. One longer detour versus shorter visits is a choice for the traveler, not an automatic rewrite. A missing event listing is not proof that nothing worthwhile exists.

## Preliminary public research examples — candidates, not selections

These examples illustrate experience-led discovery. They are not a personal itinerary, booked activity, verified availability for private dates, or proof of a working recommendation feature. No private dates, lodging, financial amounts, or reservations are included here.

### Chichibu — date-led festival candidate

Chichibu City's official festival page describes the Night Festival as occurring annually on December 2 and 3, with the main climax on the third evening, floats, performances, and fireworks. The official page reviewed still links to the detailed 2025 program. **A detailed 2026 operating program, reserved-viewing sales, and return-transport arrangements have not been confirmed in this research.** Do not turn annual recurrence into verified inventory or copy an older timetable into 2026.

Proposed fit: cultural spectacle and a dated reason to consider a destination beyond the main three. Proposed tradeoff: crowd/entry and late-return logistics need evaluation; an event-led trip may require adjusting city order. It is not assumed to be a quiet hidden attraction.

Sources checked 2026-09-19: [Chichibu City festival page](https://navi.city.chichibu.lg.jp/p_festival/1030/), [official Q&A](https://navi.city.chichibu.lg.jp/p_festival/1121/).

### Uji — hands-on tea and food candidate

Fukujuen Uji Kobo advertises a stone-mill matcha experience of approximately 40 minutes and recommends reservations because same-day space may be unavailable. Regional tourism information describes tea-related activities, food, and shopping. This supports considering a shorter cultural outing near the Kyoto part of a route; the actual workshop date, language, cost, transport, and availability must be checked before scheduling.

An assembled workshop, food, and browsing outing is a proposed combination, not a claim of one inclusive product or reservation. Operator pages contain dated price changes; use the current effective price rather than an older model-course page when preparing an actual quote.

Sources checked 2026-09-19: [Fukujuen Uji Kobo](https://www.ujikoubou.com/en/), [regional tourism activity description](https://www.kyototourism.org/en/sightseeing/24076/), [regional tea guide](https://www.kyototourism.org/en/column/8225/).

### Kinosaki Onsen, Toyooka — optional overnight candidate

Toyooka City's official tourism site describes hot-spring bathing, traditional inns, canal-side strolling, shops, and cafes. It gives approximate rail access of 2.5 hours from Kyoto and 3 hours from Osaka; these are orientation estimates, not a dated train itinerary. The tourism site's local-specialty information identifies November–March snow crab as seasonal food context, not a guarantee that a particular inn offers or includes it.

Proposed fit: an experience combining accommodation, food, cultural context, and downtime. Proposed tradeoff: additional travel and a lodging move may make it less suitable than a day trip. Bathing preferences, specific inn/meal package, operating bathhouses, dietary fit, cost, and availability are unverified. Do not assume the companion wants communal bathing, that a meal is included, or that all advertised bathhouses are open.

Sources checked 2026-09-19: [official town overview](https://visitkinosaki.com/about-kinosaki/), [official access guidance](https://visitkinosaki.com/access/), [local specialties](https://visitkinosaki.com/local-specialties/).
